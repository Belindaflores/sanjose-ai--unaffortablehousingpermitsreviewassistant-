Server validation and authorization

  • Priority: affordable, underserved, Priority housing, and additional selected categories start Unverified. Submitted priority, verification, ownership, workspace, notes, audit, timeline, and review decisions cannot bypass server-owned state. A pending claim does not raise priority. Authorized staff must record a reason for verification or rejection; supporting-document requests keep the claim Unverified. Affordable verification requires an actual uploaded evidence reference.
  • Jurisdiction: address validation runs before queue insertion or priority screening. Outside addresses return 422 without a saved permit. Incomplete, ambiguous, and shared-boundary addresses require human jurisdiction review. Address attempts and results are logged. Human confirmation requires a reason.
  • Fire/life safety: residential projects with 20 or more units require a received Fire/Life-Safety document category. Detection uses unit fields, descriptive unit counts, and category—not file name or checklist label alone. Omitted required categories are added as missing checklist items. Missing plans cause a hold, escalation, incomplete score, and blocked progression.
  • Conflicts: selected project type, unit count, permit/application category, and description are compared. Small-project classifications cannot disguise apartment-scale development; commercial/residential contradictions are also flagged, with mixed-use descriptions handled separately. Conflicts create a hold, incomplete score, and required human review. A worker must record genuinely corrected fields and a reason. Applicant corrections remain proposals until staff resolve them.
  • Workspace and object access: staff lists are filtered by assigned workspace, and mutations independently check the target record. Applicants are filtered by authenticated ownership; email filters cannot broaden access. Staff notes, internal routing, audit history, and review decisions are removed from applicant responses. Document downloads also check ownership or the staff member's workspace.
  • Safety permissions: ordinary workers cannot clear or override safety-critical flags. Only the server-assigned fire_marshal role can do so, with a reason and authenticated staff identity. A waiver does not mark the document received or make completeness 100%.
  • Audit integrity: history retains staff name/ID, timestamp, action, decision, reason, and classification before/after values. Revision checks reject stale concurrent edits rather than silently overwriting decisions.

The existing county-wide city/ZIP configuration is a prototype service geography, not an official legal permitting-jurisdiction determination. AI summaries are advisory; backend rules and authorized staff control the decisions.
